## 1. Sleep Optimization

Sleep is the foundation of Huberman's entire protocol system. He calls it "the single most important factor in health and performance."

### Morning Sunlight Protocol

- **Get outside within 30-60 minutes of waking** for 5-10 minutes of sunlight exposure (15+ minutes on overcast days)
- No sunglasses during this time -- light must reach the retinas
- Looking through a window does not count (glass filters relevant wavelengths)
- Morning sunlight triggers a cortisol pulse that sets a timer for melatonin release 14-16 hours later
- On dark/cloudy days, use a 10,000 lux light therapy lamp for 10-20 minutes

**The science:** Morning sunlight is the most powerful "zeitgeber" (time-giver) -- an environmental cue that synchronizes your biological clock. Failing to get morning light causes your clock to drift.

### Evening Sunlight Viewing

- Spend 5-10 minutes outside when the sun is low on the horizon (late afternoon/sunset)
- This provides a second anchor point for circadian rhythm
- Helps protect sleep from artificial light exposure later in the evening

### Sleep Environment & Hygiene

- **Temperature:** Keep the bedroom cool (around 65-67degF / 18-19degC)
- **Darkness:** Make the room as dark as possible
- **Consistency:** Go to bed and wake up at the same time daily (Huberman targets 10-11 PM bedtime)
- Dim lights in the evening; avoid bright overhead lights after sunset

### Sleep Supplement Stack

Taken 30-60 minutes before bed (try behavioral changes first):

| Supplement | Dosage | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Magnesium L-Threonate | 140 mg | Crosses blood-brain barrier; supports sleep quality |
| L-Theanine | 100-400 mg | Increases GABA and alpha brain waves |
| Apigenin | 50 mg | Reduces anxiety and promotes relaxation |

Occasional additions: Inositol (900 mg), GABA (100 mg), Glycine (2 g) -- rotated, not used nightly. Huberman generally avoids melatonin supplementation.

### Implementation Timeline

- **Week 1:** Morning sunlight + consistent wake time
- **Week 2:** Add evening wind-down + bedroom temperature optimization
- **Week 3:** Dial in caffeine timing; try carbs at dinner
- **Week 4:** Consider supplements if still needed

## 2. Focus & Productivity

### 90-Minute Deep Work Blocks (Ultradian Cycles)

The brain naturally operates in ~90-minute ultradian cycles. Huberman structures focused work around these cycles.

**How to implement:**

1. Start with one 90-minute deep work session each morning
2. Include a warm-up period at the start (first ~5-10 minutes may feel unfocused -- this is normal)
3. Eliminate distractions: phone away, notifications off, WiFi off if possible
4. Use low-level white noise to enhance dopamine release and maintain focus
5. Limit to 2-3 deep work sessions per day maximum
6. Take genuine breaks between sessions (unfocused, decompression time)

**Optimal timing:** Schedule sessions 4-6 hours after your body's temperature minimum (which occurs ~2 hours before your typical wake time). For most people, this means the first session starts mid-to-late morning.

### Focus-Priming Tools

- **Morning sunlight** sets circadian rhythm and primes alertness
- **Cold exposure** (1-5 min cold shower) increases catecholamines (dopamine, norepinephrine, epinephrine), boosting focus for 2-4 hours. Dopamine can increase up to 150% above baseline
- **Delayed caffeine** (90-120 min after waking) avoids afternoon crashes

### Focus Supplements (Use Intermittently)

- Caffeine (timed correctly)
- Alpha-GPC (supports acetylcholine)
- L-Tyrosine (dopamine precursor)
- Phenylethylamine (PEA)

Huberman advises not taking focus supplements every day to maintain their potency.

## 3. Exercise & Fitness

### Foundational Fitness Protocol (Weekly Split)

Huberman's protocol covers all major fitness goals across one week, targeting each muscle group twice (once directly, once indirectly).

| Day | Focus | Duration | Details |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sunday** | Zone 2 Cardio | 60-75 min | Jogging, rowing, cycling, or swimming. Should be able to hold a conversation. |
| **Monday** | Legs (Resistance) | 50-60 min + warmup | Quads, hamstrings, calves. Train largest muscle group when freshest. |
| **Tuesday** | Heat & Cold Exposure | 75-120 min | Sauna (20 min) + cold plunge (5 min), repeated 3-5 times. |
| **Wednesday** | Torso & Neck (Resistance) | 50-60 min + warmup | Chest, back, shoulders, neck. |
| **Thursday** | Moderate Cardio | 35 min | Brisk walking or light jog. |
| **Friday** | HIIT | ~20-30 min | 20-60 sec all-out sprints + 10 sec rest, 8-12 rounds. |
| **Saturday** | Arms, Neck & Calves | 50-60 min + warmup | Biceps, triceps, forearms, neck, calves. |

**Weekly cardio goal:** 180-200 minutes of Zone 2 cardio per week.

### Training Principles

- **Duration:** Keep resistance sessions to 50-60 minutes of hard work (75 min max including rest)
- **Monthly periodization:** Alternate between strength (4-8 reps, heavy) and hypertrophy (8-15 reps, moderate) each month
- **Exercise selection:** Hit each muscle from both a shortened and lengthened position; two exercises per muscle group per workout

### Cold Exposure Guidelines

- **Weekly total:** ~11 minutes of deliberate cold exposure, split across 2-4 sessions (1-5 min each)
- **Timing:** Do NOT combine with resistance training on the same day (anti-inflammatory effects can blunt hypertrophy). Wait at least 6-8 hours if needed
- **Technique:** Move limbs in the water (don't sit still) to break the thermal layer for greater stimulus
- Cold exposure significantly increases dopamine (up to 2.5x baseline) for several hours

## 4. Stress Management

### Physiological Sigh (Real-Time Stress Reset)

The fastest known way to reduce stress in real time:

1. **Double inhale** through the nose (one deep breath + one short "top-off" breath)
2. **Long, slow exhale** through the mouth
3. Repeat 1-3 cycles

**The science:** Stress increases CO2 levels, amplifying agitation. The double inhale maximally inflates the lung's alveoli (tiny air sacs), allowing the extended exhale to efficiently offload CO2. This shifts the nervous system from sympathetic (fight-or-flight) to parasympathetic (rest-and-digest) within seconds.

**Use between sets** during resistance training to lower heart rate and promote recovery.

### Non-Sleep Deep Rest (NSDR)

A term coined by Huberman as a science-friendly label for yoga nidra and related relaxation protocols.

**What it does:**
- Replenishes dopamine levels and decreases cortisol
- Creates a state of "alert calm" -- ideal for returning to focused work
- Increases learning rates when practiced for 20 minutes after a 90-minute learning session
- Overcomes the afternoon energy dip (2-5 PM slump)
- Can partially compensate for lost sleep

**How to practice:**
1. Lie down or sit comfortably with eyes closed
2. Follow a guided audio (10-30 minutes; aim for 20 min)
3. Combines body scanning, breathwork, and visualization
4. Maintain consciousness -- do not fall asleep
5. Can be done multiple times per day without affecting nighttime sleep

Huberman uses NSDR daily in the afternoon between work bouts and occasionally to fall back asleep during the night.

### Broader Stress Protocol (7 Steps)

1. Morning sunlight exposure (7-9 AM)
2. Consistent sleep schedule in a cool, dark room
3. Physiological sighs for acute stress
4. Proper nutrition (Omega-3s, Vitamin D, Magnesium)
5. 150-200 minutes of Zone 2 cardio per week
6. Sauna therapy for stress resilience
7. NSDR to reset the nervous system

## 6. Dopamine & Motivation

### The Dopamine Wave Pool Analogy

Dopamine exists in a "pool" or reservoir. Every peak in dopamine is followed by a trough below baseline. The key is managing peaks and troughs to maintain sustained motivation.

### Core Principles

1. **Avoid stacking dopamine triggers** before or during work (e.g., don't combine pre-workout + music + caffeine + phone scrolling before training -- save some for the work itself)
2. **Moderate peaks, avoid crashes:** Repeated large dopamine fluctuations deplete the reservoir. Regular, moderate increases keep the pool stable
3. **Protect the baseline:** The baseline level of dopamine determines your day-to-day motivation. Chronically spiking dopamine (social media, junk food, substances) lowers the baseline over time
4. **Refill through sleep:** Deep sleep replenishes the dopamine reservoir each night
5. **NSDR as a reset:** 10-30 minute NSDR sessions can restore dopamine levels mid-day

### Practical Dopamine Scheduling

- **Morning:** Let natural cortisol + sunlight + movement build dopamine gradually
- **Pre-work:** Use cold exposure OR caffeine (not both stacked with other stimuli)
- **During work:** Let the effort itself generate reward. Embrace friction
- **Post-work:** Allow decompression; avoid immediately seeking another dopamine spike
- **Intermittent reward:** Randomly skip rewards/stimulants to prevent adaptation

## 7. Nutrition & Fasting

### Time-Restricted Eating

Huberman has practiced intermittent fasting for over 10 years:

- **Fasting window:** 12-16 hours (typically skips breakfast)
- **Eating window:** First meal around 11 AM - 12 PM; last meal by 8:30-9 PM
- **Rule 1:** No food in the first hour after waking (ideally delay 6-8 hours)
- **Rule 2:** No food 2-3 hours before bedtime

**Tip:** Set a feeding window slightly narrower than comfortable, as people tend to underestimate when they eat.

### Caffeine Timing

- **Delay caffeine 90-120 minutes** after waking to align with natural cortisol cycles
- Preferred source: Yerba Mate (~10 AM)
- Cut all caffeine by noon to protect sleep

### Morning Hydration (Fasted State)

- Two glasses of water upon waking
- Optional: add lemon + a pinch of sea salt for electrolytes
- Salt in morning water can help reduce hunger (especially on low-carb diets)

### Meal Composition

- **First meal (late morning/lunch):** Low-carb, high-quality protein + vegetables. Keeps alertness high for afternoon work
- **Evening meal:** Include starchy carbs (rice, potatoes, pasta) to increase serotonin and tryptophan, aiding sleep quality
- **Post-workout:** Essential amino acids (EAAs)
- On intense training days: add carbs like rice or oatmeal for recovery

### Exercise Timing Relative to Food

- Work out near the end of the fasting period (after hydration and caffeine, before the first meal)
- Training in a fasted state can enhance fat oxidation and mental clarity
